# Interview

The interview is where the value is. A roadmap built on shallow answers is a generic
self-help list with the user's name on it.

## Shape of a good interview

1. **Where are you now?** Current situation, in their words.
2. **What's the constraint?** Time, money, energy, obligations.
3. **Where do you want to be?** Concrete picture, a year out.
4. **Why that?** The motivation under the goal.
5. **What's in the way?** What's stopped them before.
6. **Reflect back** and confirm before building anything.

Ask two or three questions per turn at most. Wait for answers. Follow up on anything vague —
"I want to be better with money" needs "better how — saving more, less anxiety about it,
a specific goal like a house?"

Use tappable options for choices ("how many hours a week — under 5, 5–10, 10–20, 20+")
and open questions for anything about motivation. Options for "why do you want this?"
put words in the user's mouth.

## Constraint questions — ask every first session

- Realistically, how many hours a week can go toward this? (Not ideally — realistically.)
- What else is competing for that time right now?
- Any hard deadlines or fixed commitments in the next year?
- What's the budget, if the plan costs money — courses, equipment, time off?

If answers are optimistic, probe once: "What happened to your free time last month?" People
plan with their ideal week and live their actual one.

## Per-area questions

Pick from these — don't ask them all. Follow the conversation.

### Career and skills

- What do you do now, and which parts do you enjoy versus tolerate?
- What does the job you want in a year actually involve day to day?
- Is this about the work itself, the money, the people, the growth, or the security?
- What have you done that you're proud of — and what did it have in common with other things
  you've enjoyed?
- Who's doing what you want to do? What did their path look like?

### Learning

- What's the goal the learning serves? Learning for a job, for a project, or for its own sake
  get different plans.
- How do you learn best — building things, courses, reading, being taught?
- What have you started and dropped before, and why did it stop?
- What do you already know that the new thing builds on?

That last one matters for ordering. A roadmap that starts from zero when the person has
adjacent skills wastes months and feels patronising.

### Personal life — habits, health, relationships

- What would a good ordinary week look like?
- Which habit, if it stuck, would make other things easier?
- What's draining you that you'd like less of?
- Who do you want to spend more time with?

Tread lightly. Let the user set the depth. If they give a short answer about relationships,
don't dig — they've told you where the boundary is.

### Money and big decisions

- Is there a specific goal — a purchase, a safety net, a date — or a general feeling?
- What's the decision you keep putting off?
- What would you do if the money side were solved?

Frame outputs as options and trade-offs. Don't recommend specific products or investments.

## Surfacing tension

Goals in different areas often conflict. Common ones:

- Career switch versus time with family.
- Aggressive saving versus learning that costs money.
- Many goals, few hours.

Name the tension plainly and let the user choose: "These two pull against each other with
five hours a week. Which matters more for the next 90 days?" Choosing is the useful part.
A roadmap that avoids the choice just delays it.

## Reflecting back

Before building, summarise in a few sentences: current state, constraint, goal, the why, the
main obstacle. Ask "have I got that right?" and fix what's wrong. This step catches most
misunderstandings and makes the user feel heard, which makes them more likely to follow the
plan.

## When to stop

If the conversation turns heavy — grief, burnout, hopelessness — stop interviewing. Respond
to what they've said. A roadmap is the wrong response to a person in pain. Suggest talking to
someone they trust or a professional, and let them decide whether to continue another time.
