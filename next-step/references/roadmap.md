# Building the roadmap

## Three horizons

- **This week** — 1–3 actions, each small enough to finish in one sitting. The most
  important part of the roadmap. Without it, the plan stays an intention.
- **90 days** — 2–4 milestones per focus area. Concrete and checkable.
- **12 months** — the destination, stated as an outcome, plus the 1–2 milestones that prove
  you're on the way.

Don't plan further than 12 months in detail. Plans that far out are guesses, and the check-in
process will reshape them anyway.

## Milestone rules

**Checkable.** Someone else could verify it happened.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Learn Go | Build and deploy a REST API in Go with auth, live at a public URL |
| Get healthier | Walk 30 minutes, 4 days a week, for 6 consecutive weeks |
| Save more | Build a 3-month emergency fund — ₹X by March |
| Network more | Have coffee with 6 people working in backend roles by December |

**Sized to hours.** Estimate effort per milestone and check the total against weekly hours.
When it doesn't fit, cut or extend — don't hope. Show the arithmetic to the user when it's
close: "These three milestones need about 120 hours; at 6 a week that's 20 weeks, not 12."

**Ordered by dependency.** Put prerequisites first. Say which milestone unblocks which.

**Built on what they already have.** Existing skills shorten paths. Don't start from zero.

**Includes a reason.** One line on why each milestone matters, taken from the interview.
When motivation dips in month two, "why am I doing this?" needs an answer on the page.

## Learning roadmaps specifically

- Order by what unblocks the goal, not by textbook order.
- Favour building over consuming. Each learning milestone should end with something made.
- Suggest resource *types* and well-established starting points; don't pile up links.
  Three good resources beat fifteen.
- Where recency matters — tools, frameworks, job market — search before recommending rather
  than relying on what might be outdated.

## Cross-area links

Note where areas connect: "The 90-day portfolio project (learning) is what makes the job
applications (career) possible." A roadmap that shows the links reads as one plan rather
than four to-do lists.

## Keeping it usable

A roadmap nobody opens is a failure. Keep the master roadmap to what fits on roughly one
screen per area; detail lives in session notes.
